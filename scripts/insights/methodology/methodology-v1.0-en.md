## 0. Purpose and posture
This document sets one method for every VisionSeek strategic report, index, ledger, census and playbook. We publish it **before** our reports so that readers can judge each report against rules fixed in advance.

**Facts, not directives.** VisionSeek reports build a fact base about technological capability. They take no policy positions, do not judge governments' choices, and contain no policy recommendations. Where a report states implications, they are written as conditional, evidence-linked observations ("if X, the data show Y"), not as advice [VisionSeek]. This follows an established practice: the McKinsey Global Institute describes its role as "developing a fact base" and states that it does not give "any policy prescriptions" [Source: https://www.mckinsey.com/about-us/new-at-mckinsey-blog/how-the-mckinsey-global-institute-is-changing-the-way-we-think-about-work-gender-equality-and-more].

## 1. Definitions and scope
### 1.1 Core principle: ownership, not usage
VisionSeek measures whether a capability is **owned**, not whether a technology is **used**. These definitions are consistent with VisionSeek's Localization Doctrine:
- **Usage**: operating technology that is designed, controlled or maintained elsewhere. A data centre on our soil is use.
- **Ownership**: control of the operating software, the data standards, and the trained engineers who design, adapt and sustain the capability.
- **Ownership is a sequence, not a purchase.**
- **Export is what proves ownership.**
- **Verify, or it didn't happen**: only verified ownership counts, never announced ownership.
- **Absorption institution**: an institution with an explicit mandate to absorb transferred technology.

### 1.2 Fixed framework: the Ownership Ladder
Every report places capability on the same five rungs, for each country and each technology:

| Rung | Name | Working definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Use | Imports and operates foreign-designed technology. Maintenance and upgrades depend on the supplier. |
| 2 | Assemble | Assembles, installs or integrates foreign-designed components locally. |
| 3 | Adapt | Local teams modify designs, software or processes and hold the documentation needed to do so. |
| 4 | Design | Local entities hold design authority, originate designs and own the resulting intellectual property. |
| 5 | Export / Standard | Locally designed products or services are certified or sold abroad, or local actors help shape international standards. |

Rules:
- **(a)** The ladder is fixed across all reports and editions. Any change requires a new major version of this methodology and a published crosswalk to the old version.
- **(b)** A rung is assigned only on evidence that meets the tier requirements in §2.
- **(c)** Where the evidence is insufficient, the cell reads **"Not assessable"**. We never guess a rung.
- **(d)** Every rung assignment states the date of its evidence.

### 1.3 Other standard terms
- **Capability**: the ability of entities based in a country to perform a technical function (design, produce, operate, maintain, certify) without depending on a single foreign party for that function.
- **Critical technology areas**: semiconductors; artificial intelligence; cloud and data; 5G/6G; robotics; clean energy and batteries; biotechnology and health technology.
- **Announced vs delivered**: an announcement is a claim by a party. Delivery requires independent evidence (§2).
- **Local**: each report states which definition it uses (place of incorporation, equity ownership, residence of inventors or engineers, or nationality) and does not mix them.

### 1.4 Required scope statement
Each report opens with:
- geography;
- technologies;
- time window;
- unit of analysis;
- what is out of scope;
- the ladder rungs assessed.

## 2. Data sources and reliability tiers
Every data point carries a tier label in the published data file.

| Tier | Type | Examples (types, not endorsements) | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Official registers and primary records | Patent registers, regulator certifications, company and court filings, audited annual reports, official statistics | Can support a claim on its own |
| **B** | Primary institutional statements | Government and company press releases, official strategy documents | Supports claims about *what was stated*. Delivery claims need corroboration |
| **C** | Peer-reviewed or established research | Peer-reviewed papers, intergovernmental reports, research organizations with published methods | Context and benchmarks, cited to page or section |
| **D** | Reputable press and industry reports | Established news outlets; industry reports without a published method | Leads and corroboration only. Never the sole support for a number |
| **E** | Unverified or anonymous sources | Sites of unknown ownership, unattributed trackers, social media | Not citable. Used only as leads to trace back to Tiers A–C |

Rules:
1. Every number has a Tier A–C source, or two independent Tier D sources with any disagreement stated.
2. Tier B content is reported as a statement ("X announced…"), not as a fact of delivery.
3. When official sources disagree, both are reported.
4. Each source record holds: the URL or reference, the access date, how it was accessed (full or partial read), the tier, and the exact claim it supports.
5. Web sources are archived at the time of access wherever possible.

## 3. Handling missing data
1. **No silent imputation.** Missing values in headline results read "Not available".
2. **Coverage is reported.** For each indicator we report data coverage and the dates of the data.
3. **Labelled estimates.** An unavoidable estimate is labelled **[Estimate]**, its method is shown in the annex, it is given as a range, and it is excluded from any ranking.
4. **"Not found" describes our search, not the world.** Gaps are worded as "not identified in the sources searched (listed)", never as "does not exist".
5. **"Not assessable", not ranked low.** A country or technology with insufficient data is marked "Not assessable" rather than ranked low.
6. **"What we don't know."** Every report includes a page with this title.
7. **Dependencies stated.** Where a conclusion depends on a missing item, the report says so explicitly.

## 4. Review
### 4.1 Independent internal verification
An independent internal verification function, separate from the authoring team, reviews every draft. It checks that:
- every number has been traced back to its source and re-read;
- tier labels are correct and no Tier E source is cited;
- ladder assignments match the evidence;
- the prior-coverage check (§8) is complete and logged;
- the text contains no policy-recommendation language;
- the Arabic and English versions are consistent.

The function produces a written review memo recording each issue and how it was resolved.

### 4.2 External expert review
Reports are reviewed by external experts in the following roles, as relevant:
- a domain expert for each technology covered;
- a patent and intellectual-property analyst;
- a statistics or labour-market methodologist;
- a legal reviewer for contract-related work (VisionSeek does not give legal advice);
- a policy specialist from outside the region assessed, to test for bias;
- a data and statistics reviewer for any composite index.

Reviewers sign a conflict-of-interest declaration. Combined internal and external peer review is an established practice: Chatham House states that all its reports and research papers "are externally and internally peer reviewed, fact-checked and tested for originality" [Source: https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/our-editorial-standards].

## 5. Independence and conflict-of-interest statement
> VisionSeek self-funds its research. It accepts no paid commissions and no funding from any party for its reports. VisionSeek alone determines its research questions, methods and conclusions. No government, company or other party may approve, alter or delay a report. Authors and reviewers declare any financial, employment, advisory or personal relationship with the entities assessed. Where a material conflict exists, the person is recused from the relevant work and the recusal is recorded. Entities assessed may be offered a check on the factual accuracy of data about themselves before publication. They may correct facts, not conclusions.

## 6. Quality standards
RAND's standards for research quality are framed around six principles: **Engagement, Inclusion, Relevance, Rigor, Transparency and Legitimacy**. They were first launched in 1997 and updated in 1999, 2003, 2009 and 2022 [Source: https://www.rand.org/about/standards.html]. VisionSeek adopts the same six principles as written requirements:

| Standard | RAND's summary [Source: same URL] | VisionSeek requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement | "we interact with those who have a stake in how our research is conducted, interpreted, and applied" | Scope and questions are documented before research starts. Assessed entities are offered a factual-accuracy check (§5). |
| Inclusion | "we include all important perspectives throughout the research process" | Contrary evidence and alternative interpretations are reported. At least one reviewer comes from outside the region assessed. |
| Relevance | "we seek to inform and influence effective and timely solutions to important policy problems" | The scope statement explains why the question matters now, without policy prescriptions (§0). |
| Rigor | "objective analyses grounded in a clear purpose using sound logic and the most appropriate theories, methods, and data sources available" | A fixed ladder, tiered sources, replicable indicator definitions, and every claim traceable to its source. |
| Transparency | document "purpose, scope, sources of support, assumptions, activities, data, methods, results, limitations…" | A method annex, a data file and a source list are published with every report, along with a funding statement and a limitations page. |
| Legitimacy | "we conduct research ethically, avoid conflicts of interest, and maintain independence and objectivity" | Independence statement (§5), conflict-of-interest declarations, and the corrections policy (§7). |

Additional rules:
- No number, name or source is ever invented.
- Every exhibit carries its source beneath it.
- Every claim is traceable to evidence or labelled as VisionSeek analysis.

## 7. Corrections, updates and declared limitations
- **Corrections.** Factual errors are corrected promptly. The corrected document carries a dated correction note stating what changed. Silent edits are not permitted. The public corrections channel opens with the release of the first report.
- **Versioning.** Every report and dataset carries a version number (MAJOR.MINOR). A minor version covers corrections and data updates. A major version covers a change of method or ladder definitions and is published with a crosswalk to the previous version.
- **Version log.** Each report annex records the version, date, change and reason.
- **Updates.** Recurring products state their update cycle. Past editions remain available.
- **Declared limitations.** Every report states its limitations, including:
  - gaps in data coverage;
  - reliance on public sources;
  - the languages searched;
  - the date of the evidence;
  - definitional choices, such as what counts as "local";
  - the fact that announced deals may not be fully observable.

## 8. Prior-coverage check before any "first" or "gap" claim
No report claims to be "the first" or "the only", or to fill "a gap", unless this protocol has been completed and logged:
1. **State the claim precisely**: topic, geography, method and period.
2. **Write a search plan in advance.** At minimum it covers:
   - general web search in Arabic, English and other relevant languages;
   - academic databases;
   - publications of intergovernmental organizations;
   - regional and international think tanks;
   - consultancy and industry reports;
   - national statistics and ministry sites;
   - dissertations and conference proceedings.
3. **Keep a log** of every query, database and date and the results reviewed. List the closest existing works and how they differ.
4. **Second searcher.** The independent internal verification function repeats the search independently.
5. **Expert check.** External domain experts are asked whether they know of prior work.
6. **Wording rule: "not found" does not mean "does not exist".** The permitted wording is: *"To our knowledge, after the search documented in Annex X, we did not identify…"*. "First" or "only" is used only after steps 1–5. It is withdrawn by a correction if prior work comes to light.

## 9. Standard report structure
Every report follows this structure:
1. key numbers page;
2. executive summary;
3. scope statement;
4. findings with numbered exhibits, each with its source beneath it;
5. "What we don't know";
6. method annex;
7. source list with tiers;
8. version log;
9. independence and funding statement.
