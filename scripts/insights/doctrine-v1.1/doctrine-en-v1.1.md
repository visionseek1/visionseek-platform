# The Localization Doctrine
## From Using Technology to Owning It: What Korea Proves Is Possible for the Arab World
*VisionSeek Insights. Feeds the Arab Technological Capability Index and the Incheon Dialogue (April 2027).*

## This is our moment
For two generations the Arab world has been an excellent *buyer* of technology. We bought the best networks, the best power plants and the best devices, and every purchase renewed our dependence. The question was never whether we could afford technology. It was whether we would ever *own* it.

Korea answers that question. In 1971 it founded its first science and technology university under a special law, with a USAID loan of six million dollars [Verified #1]. In 1983 Samsung developed a 64Kb DRAM, making Korea the third country in the world with advanced VLSI technology [Verified #4]. By 1992 Samsung held the world's largest DRAM market share [Verified #4]. Korea is not the subject of this report. It is the **proof** that a country that starts as a buyer can become an owner within one working lifetime.

For the first time, several of the conditions behind Korea's leap are present in our region at once. This is the time we have waited for. The danger is that we spend it buying again.

## Executive summary: five conclusions
1. **Ownership is a sequence, not a purchase.** Korea moved from import to assembly, then absorption, then its own design, then setting the standard. Foreign technology was the *first* step, never the last [Inference, from the cases below].
2. **The decisive asset is the "absorption institution."** KAIST was created by special law with an explicit national mission [Verified #1], and ETRI carried Korea from telephone switches to CDMA to 6G [Verified #3]. Arab states have invested in purchases. Few have built an institution that absorbs what they buy [Inference].
3. **Export is what proves ownership.** The Korean APR1400 reactor counted as owned once it was certified abroad [Verified #18] and running in the UAE [Verified #19]. Even then, an intellectual-property dispute over its origins had to be settled [Verified #20] [Inference on the lesson].
4. **Settle rights at the door.** Korea's battery leaders ended up before a US trade tribunal over trade secrets [Verified #17], and its reactor exporter had to settle with Westinghouse [Verified #20]. IP left unresolved when technology is transferred comes back when it is exported [Inference].
5. **Integrity is infrastructure.** The landmark 2005 Korean stem-cell paper in *Science* was retracted [Verified #24]. Faked capability destroys trust faster than real capability builds it, so an Arab capability index must measure ownership that has been verified, not announced [Inference].

## Why now: what has actually changed
- **National visions with diversification as an explicit goal.** Saudi Arabia's Cabinet approved Vision 2030 on 25 April 2016 [Verified #25], and the document commits to diversifying the economy [Verified #26]. Egypt launched Vision 2030 in 2016 and has since updated it [Verified #27]. Visions built on purchases stall; visions built on absorption compound [Inference].
- **Arab AI institutions now exist.** The UAE made AI a national priority in 2017, appointed the world's first Minister of State for AI, and in 2019 announced MBZUAI, described as the first graduate-level, research-based AI university, whose first class started in September 2020 [Verified #28]. TII is part of Abu Dhabi's Advanced Technology Research Council [Verified #29]. G42 describes itself as "born in Abu Dhabi, operating globally" [Verified #30]. In 2025 Saudi Arabia's PIF launched HUMAIN to work across the AI value chain, including a multimodal Arabic large language model [Verified #31]. These are the first Arab counterparts to Korea's KAIST/ETRI layer [Inference].
- **The AI window is open.** AI capability is still forming, and entry into applied, sector-specific and Arabic-language models costs far less than leading-edge chip fabs [Inference].
- **Governments are redrawing supply chains.** The US CHIPS and Science Act provides $52.7 billion and a 25% investment tax credit, and the White House says the US relies on East Asia for 75% of chip production [Verified #8]. Korea expanded its own semiconductor tax support in 2023 [Verified #7]. When the leaders subsidize relocation, partners look for new, stable sites rich in energy and capital, and the Arab region is one of them [Inference].
- **Korea is looking at us.** Korea's 6G strategy names the Middle East as a priority region for its digital exports [Verified #11]. A partner that wants our market is a partner that can be asked for absorption terms [Inference].
- **A Korean ownership proof already stands on Arab soil.** Barakah has four APR-1400 units, construction began in 2012, and Unit 1 entered commercial operation in April 2021 [Verified #19].

## 1. Semiconductors
**What Korea did.** Samsung entered the field by acquiring Hankook Semiconductor in 1974 [Verified #4]. Its founder made the "Tokyo declaration" on 8 February 1983 [Verified #5]. That same year it developed the 64Kb DRAM [Verified #4], having taken basic DRAM technology from Micron under contract, and its trainees had only limited access during the transfer [Press #6]. In 1992 it developed the industry's first 64Mb DRAM and reached the No. 1 DRAM market share [Verified #4]. ETRI, the state research institute, developed a 4M DRAM in 1989 [Verified #3].
**Mechanism.** License, then absorb by force of necessity when the licensor holds back, then leap ahead in *one* narrow segment (memory) where manufacturing discipline beats design breadth [Inference]. A state research institute ran in parallel with the firms [Verified #3; Inference on the role].
**What failed.** Market shares are outside the scope of this edition [Gap]. The risks of concentrating on memory, such as price cycles, are an inference [Inference].
**Arab transfer.** What applies: choose one segment, and write absorption clauses into every licence. What does not: copying a leading-edge fab, because Korea's window of the 1980s is closed [Inference].
**The open opportunity for Arabs now:** packaging and testing, chip design for our own sectors (energy, water), and power semiconductors for the grid [Inference; needs a feasibility study].

## 2. Artificial Intelligence
**What Korea did.** On 24 August 2023 Naver unveiled HyperCLOVA X, its own generative AI, together with the CLOVA X assistant and CUE: search [Verified #9]. It is a domestic platform built on its own data and infrastructure.
**Mechanism.** A national platform company owns the language, search and data layer [Inference].
**What failed.** Outside the scope of this edition [Gap].
**Arab transfer.** What applies: language sovereignty, since Arabic is a market large enough to justify models we own [Inference]. What does not: competing on frontier compute alone [Inference].
**The open opportunity for Arabs now:** Arabic models for health, education, public services, law and heritage. Institutions that can carry this already exist [Verified #28, #29, #31; the opportunity is Inference].

## 3. Cloud & Data
**What Korea did.** Korea runs a Cloud Security Assurance Program (CSAP) for cloud providers serving the public sector. MSIT and the National Intelligence Service have announced that it will be merged into a single NIS-led verification system, fully in place in the second half of 2027 [Verified #12].
**Mechanism.** The state sets the rules for entering the public market. Sovereignty is exercised through certification, not ownership of every data centre [Inference].
**Arab transfer.** A data centre on our soil is *use*. Owning the operating software, the data standards and the trained engineers is *ownership* [Inference].
**The open opportunity for Arabs now:** sovereign-cloud certification that requires local operations and engineering teams, so that every data centre becomes a site of absorption [Inference].

## 4. 5G / 6G
**What Korea did.** ETRI developed the TDX switch in 1986, commercialized the world's first CDMA mobile system in 1995, and went on to WiBro in 2004 and LTE-Advanced in 2010 [Verified #3]. On 3 April 2019 Korea achieved the world's first smartphone-based commercial 5G service [Verified #10]. In February 2023 MSIT launched the K-Network 2030 strategy. It runs a preliminary feasibility study for KRW 625.3 billion of 6G R&D, plans a Pre-6G Vision Fest in 2026, and targets a 5G patent share above 30%, up from 25.9% according to IPlytics as cited by MSIT [Verified #11].
**Mechanism.** A state lab co-develops with industry, the national operators act as first buyers, and the state then sets the standards agenda [Verified #3, #11; Inference on the mechanism].
**What failed.** Outside the scope of this edition [Gap].
**Arab transfer.** What applies: the state as a *demanding first customer*, with local R&D obligations. What does not: building a full national equipment vendor [Inference].
**The open opportunity for Arabs now:** 6G is still before standardization, so now is the time to contribute to standards and testbeds rather than buy them later. Korea itself is seeking Middle East partners [Verified #11; Inference].

## 5. Robotics
**What Korea did.** Rainbow Robotics was founded in 2011 by researchers from KAIST's Humanoid Robot Research Center, the team behind Hubo, Korea's first two-legged walking robot. Samsung bought 14.7% in 2023 for KRW 86.8 billion and moved to 35% [Verified #13]. Korea's Fair Trade Commission approved the deal on 5 March 2025 [Verified #14].
**Mechanism.** A university lab becomes a spin-out, which is then acquired by a national champion, closing the capital loop [Inference].
**What failed.** Outside the scope of this edition [Gap].
**The open opportunity for Arabs now:** robots for our own hard problems, such as heat, desert logistics, construction, hospitals, and crowd services during Hajj and Umrah [Inference].

## 6. Clean Energy & Batteries
**What Korea did.** LG Energy Solution dates its lithium-ion research to 1992 and the mass production of cylindrical lithium-ion cells to 1999 [Verified #15]. LG Chem's own archive dates the start of battery research to 1995 [Verified #16], so the two official sources differ. In nuclear, the US NRC certified the APR1400 design with effect from 19 September 2019 [Verified #18], and four units were built at Barakah in the UAE [Verified #19].
**What failed.** SK and LG fought a battery trade-secret dispute before the US International Trade Commission, which they settled in April 2021 [Verified #17]. Westinghouse and KEPCO/KHNP settled an IP dispute in January 2025 on confidential terms [Verified #20]. Lesson: **IP left unresolved at transfer returns at export** [Inference].
**Arab transfer.** Barakah shows that Arab states can host ownership. The next step is to require Arab engineering roles in operation and design [Inference].
**The open opportunity for Arabs now:** grid-scale storage for solar, and processing battery materials where the region has energy and capital [Inference].

## 7. Biotech & Health Tech
**What Korea did.** The EMA recommended Remsima (Celltrion), together with Inflectra, as the first two monoclonal-antibody biosimilars [Verified #21], and Remsima was authorised across the EU on 10 September 2013 [Verified #22]. Samsung Biologics opened its Plant 5 in April 2025, bringing total capacity to 784,000 litres [Verified #23].
**Mechanism.** Enter through *manufacturing* to Western regulatory standards, then move up toward innovation [Inference].
**What failed.** The 2005 *Science* stem-cell paper was retracted [Verified #24].
**The open opportunity for Arabs now:** a regional base for biosimilars and vaccine fill-finish serving Arab and African markets, with ethical governance of genomic data within the bounds of Sharia and medical ethics [Inference].

## The Localization Doctrine: eight principles [Inference]
1. **Buy to learn, never buy to keep.** Every contract names what we will absorb, who absorbs it, and by when.
2. **Build the absorbing institution before the deal.**
3. **Choose one narrow beachhead.** Korea won memory, not all chips.
4. **The state is first customer and standard-setter, not operator.**
5. **Keep the core (R&D, pilot lines, quality control) at home in every joint venture.** A VisionSeek proposal, not a description of Korea.
6. **Settle IP at the door.**
7. **Export is the exam.**
8. **Verify, or it didn't happen.**

**For the Arab Technological Capability Index** we propose a five-step ladder for each country and technology: *Use → Assemble → Adapt → Design → Export/Standard* [Inference].

## A call to action
Korea started its first research university with a six-million-dollar loan [Verified #1]. We have capital, energy, young people and, for the first time, our own AI institutions [Verified #28–31]. What we have lacked is a doctrine: the discipline to turn every purchase into a lesson and every lesson into ownership.

To Arab decision-makers: in your next technology deal, ask one question. *What will we own when this contract ends?* If there is no answer, the deal is not finished.

Let the Incheon Dialogue in April 2027 be where we start, and the moment the Arab world stopped buying the future and began building it.
